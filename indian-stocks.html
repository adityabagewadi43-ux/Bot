Why Indian Stocks Move

Strong quarterly earnings can trigger re-rating and momentum continuation.
Institutional buying in large caps often supports index trend.
Sector rotation can push money from IT to banks, energy, autos, or PSU names.
Newsflow from policy, capex, and reforms can extend trends.
Weak guidance can break momentum even after good results.
Rupee weakness, crude spikes, or global risk-off can pressure sentiment.
Overextended charts can reverse sharply at resistance zones.
Low liquidity names can be unpredictable and harder to trade.

Market Structure

Large Caps

These are the main index drivers. They usually have deeper liquidity, tighter spreads, and attract institutional flow.

Mid Caps

Mid caps often offer stronger momentum but can be more volatile. Earnings quality and valuation matter more here.

Small Caps

Small caps can move fast when sentiment is strong, but they can also reverse violently if breadth weakens.

Sectors

Banking, IT, energy, autos, pharma, metals, FMCG, infra, and PSU themes often rotate depending on macro conditions.

Pro Trader Checklist

• Check trend on the index first, then sector leadership, then stock-specific setup.

• Watch volume confirmation on breakouts and avoid chasing stretched candles.

• Use support/resistance, previous day high/low, VWAP, and opening range as practical levels.

• Respect event risk like RBI policy, earnings, budget announcements, and global market openings.

• Align trade size with volatility. Liquidity matters more in Indian markets than many beginners realize.

Indian Market Notes

• Nifty and Sensex are the key reference points for broad market direction.

• Oil, banks, IT, and PSU names can all lead at different times depending on macro and flows.
