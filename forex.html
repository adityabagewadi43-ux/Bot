Forex Basics

WHAT IS FOREX
Currency Exchange
Forex means buying one currency while selling another. Every trade is a pair, like EUR/USD or USD/INR.
WHY FOREX EXISTS
Trade & Hedging
Companies, travelers, importers, exporters, governments, and investors need forex for settlement and risk control.
WHAT MOVES PRICE
Rates & News
Inflation, interest rates, central banks, trade balance, geopolitics, and risk sentiment move currencies.
PRICE UNITS
Pips / Lots
Pips measure movement. Lots measure trade size. Spread is the cost between bid and ask prices.

Why Forex Matters

It supports international trade because payments across countries need conversion into other currencies.
It helps exporters and importers hedge against currency swings that can affect profits.
It gives traders access to one of the most liquid markets in the world.
It reflects macroeconomics in real time, so it is useful for both analysis and speculation.
It can be highly leveraged, which makes losses faster than many beginners expect.
Major news can create sudden spikes, slippage, and widened spreads.
Low-liquidity hours can be deceptive and can produce false breakouts.
Poor risk management can wipe out accounts even when the trade direction is right.

Major Currency Pairs

EUR/USD

The most watched pair globally. It is heavily influenced by US and Eurozone rate expectations, inflation, and central-bank language.

GBP/USD

Often more volatile than EUR/USD because sterling reacts strongly to UK data and surprise policy shifts.

USD/JPY

A major macro pair driven by US yields, Japan policy, risk sentiment, and safe-haven demand.

USD/INR

Important for India. It reacts to RBI policy, crude oil, import demand, portfolio flows, and the US dollar trend.

Why Some Pairs Fluctuate More

Lower liquidity usually means bigger swings, especially in exotic pairs.
Economic releases create sharp volatility when expectations are far from the actual result.
Session overlap, especially London and New York, often increases momentum and volume [web:18][web:19].
Pairs tied to interest-rate changes can move quickly around central bank meetings.
Pairs with thin order books can gap or spike without much warning.
News during illiquid Asian hours can produce fake breakouts.
High leverage magnifies every small move into a larger equity swing.
Sudden policy intervention can reverse a strong trend in minutes.

Common Candlestick Patterns

Doji

Shows indecision. Often useful near support or resistance when direction is unclear.

Bullish Engulfing

Can indicate a possible reversal when buyers overpower the previous candle strongly.

Bearish Engulfing

Often warns of a downside reversal after a strong upward move.

Hammer

Can signal rejection of lower prices after sellers failed to keep control.

Shooting Star

May appear after an uptrend and can suggest exhaustion near resistance.

Inside Bar

Often reflects contraction before expansion, useful for breakout setups.

Expert Trader Checklist

• Start from the higher timeframe first, then move down to the entry timeframe.

• Watch trend, momentum, liquidity, and event risk together, not as separate ideas.

• Use support, resistance, prior highs/lows, VWAP, moving averages, and session opens as practical levels.

• Keep position size smaller when volatility expands or a major announcement is near.

• Treat leverage as a tool, not a strategy.

Risk Management

Use stop-loss orders to define risk before entering the trade.
Risk only a small fixed portion of capital on each trade.
Avoid overtrading during news spikes and thin sessions.
Do not hold oversized leveraged positions through unknown event risk.
Do not average losses without a clear plan.
Do not confuse a lucky win with a repeatable edge.

Forex Glossary

Bid is the price buyers are willing to pay.
Ask is the price sellers are willing to accept.
Spread is the difference between bid and ask.
Pip is the standard unit of movement in many pairs.
Lot is the trade size.
Leverage increases exposure using less capital.
Margin is the deposit required to open a trade.
Volatility measures how fast price changes.
Liquidity measures how easily a trade can be executed.
Hedge reduces risk from exchange-rate movement.
