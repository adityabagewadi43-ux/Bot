01 / Market foundations

Oil is physical. Price is a story.

Crude is a globally traded energy feedstock. Its price forms continuously as real-world barrels, future expectations and risk premiums collide.

O
01

What is crude oil?

Crude oil is an unrefined mixture of hydrocarbons extracted from underground reservoirs. Refineries transform different grades into transport fuels, petrochemicals, asphalt and other products. Grade quality, sulphur content and regional delivery access all matter.

From wellhead to price
Upstream→Pipeline / tanker→Refinery→Products→Consumer demand
02

How oil trading works

Producers, refiners, airlines, funds and merchants use futures and options to lock in prices or express a view. Benchmarks make physical barrels comparable; derivatives make risk transferable.

03

Spot vs. futures

Spot reflects a barrel for prompt delivery. Futures price delivery in a specific future month. The curve can reward storage (contango) or immediate supply (backwardation).

04

Benchmark contrast

EUROPE / GLOBAL WATERBORNE

Brent

A light, sweet North Sea benchmark used to price a large share of internationally traded crude. It is especially sensitive to seaborne supply and global risk.

UNITED STATES / CUSHING DELIVERY

WTI

A light, sweet U.S. benchmark tied to delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma. It is particularly shaped by domestic shale output, pipeline flows and U.S. inventories.

04 / Extreme tape

When oil broke the range.

Outlier moves are useful case studies: they reveal which part of the market—supply, demand, storage or liquidity—was under pressure.

1973–74

Embargo spike

Arab oil embargo constricted supply to key importers and reset the world’s energy-risk premium.

↑ roughly 4×
1979–80

Iranian supply shock

Revolution and war fears tightened barrels, amplifying a second major energy crisis.

↑ sharp surge
1990

Gulf War shock

Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait drove an abrupt premium, followed by a pullback as disruption fears eased.

↑ then retraced
2008

Peak & collapse

Demand and speculation met financial crisis deleveraging: a historic high rapidly became a historic drawdown.

↓ violent reversal
2020

Negative WTI print

Pandemic demand destruction collided with storage scarcity near the futures delivery point.

↓ below zero
05 / Price engine

Ten drivers to monitor.

No single input sets price. The edge comes from watching how drivers reinforce—or cancel—each other.

01

OPEC+ policy

Quota discipline or supply restoration can shift global balances fast.

02

Geopolitics

Conflict, sanctions and security threats add a risk premium to barrels.

03

U.S. shale

Flexible shale drilling helps determine the non-OPEC supply response.

04

Global demand

Mobility, industry and emerging-market growth set consumption tempo.

05

Recession risk

Falling activity can trigger demand destruction and lower refinery runs.

06

Dollar strength

A stronger USD can make dollar-priced oil less accessible abroad.

07

Rates & inflation

Rates shape growth expectations; inflation changes real commodity demand.

08

Strategic reserves

Government releases or refill programs alter visible inventory flows.

09

Weather cycles

Hurricanes, winter heating and driving season change regional demand.

10

Shipping routes

Chokepoint disruption raises freight, delays deliveries and tightens prompt supply.

06 / Trader academy

Build a process, not a prediction.

A compact playbook for turning a chart and a headline into a structured oil-market question.

Four lenses for the oil chart

01
Start with the regimeMap higher highs/higher lows versus a range or downtrend before acting on one candle.
02
Make breakouts earn trustLook for a close through a well-tested level plus participation, not just an intraday wick.
03
Name the shockSupply loss often tightens prompt spreads; demand destruction tends to weaken products and outright price.
04
Design the risk before entryDefine invalidation, position size, maximum loss and the event that can change your thesis.
Risk protocol

Trade the setup.
Respect the barrel.

  • Use a stop based on market structure, not a desired dollar amount.
  • Scale position size down when volatility expands.
  • Know the contract unit, tick value, expiry and delivery mechanics.
  • Treat scheduled OPEC, inventory and central-bank events as scenario points.
  • Never confuse a convincing narrative with a confirmed trend.
