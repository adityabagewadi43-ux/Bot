Oil Market Basics

WHAT DRIVES OIL
Supply + Demand
Production cuts, refinery demand, seasonal driving, sanctions, and storage reports all affect price action.
MAIN BENCHMARKS
WTI / Brent
WTI is the U.S. benchmark. Brent is the global benchmark and often trades differently due to logistics and regional demand.
OIL STOCKS
Energy Equities
Integrated majors, drillers, service firms, pipeline operators, and ETFs often move with crude but not always at the same speed.

Why Oil Moves

OPEC+ output decisions can tighten or loosen supply quickly.
Summer driving season can lift gasoline demand and refinery runs.
Geopolitical disruptions can create sudden risk premiums.
Inventory draws often support bullish sentiment.
Demand slowdowns can pressure crude and energy shares.
Inventory builds may signal weak consumption.
Higher rates can affect growth expectations and fuel demand.
Sharp swings in oil can make leveraged exposure risky.

Oil Market Structure

Upstream

Exploration and production companies drill for and produce crude oil and natural gas. They usually benefit most when crude prices rise.

Midstream

Pipelines, transport, storage, and logistics move crude and refined products. These firms often focus more on volume and fees than spot prices.

Downstream

Refineries turn crude into gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and chemicals. Refining margins matter more than crude alone.

Oil Services

Drilling, seismic, rigs, and field services support the full oil supply chain. They can be highly cyclical and sensitive to capex trends.

Trading View

INVENTORY DATA

RISK STYLE
Moderate to High
Oil can trend cleanly but also gap sharply on headlines, making risk controls important.
BEST USE
Macro + Swing
Oil often works better with broader macro and inventory context than with pure intraday noise.

Market Notes

• WTI and Brent are benchmarks, not individual stocks. Oil stocks such as XOM and CVX react to crude, margins, production, and capital spending.

• ETFs like USO can track oil futures behavior more directly than energy equities, which means they may diverge from stock-based oil plays.
