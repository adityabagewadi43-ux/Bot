GOLD 101

Understand the market before the move

Gold trades both as a physical asset and as a macroeconomic expression of trust, risk, and monetary policy.

THE METAL

Gold is part currency,
part commodity, part hedge.

Gold is a globally priced precious metal measured in troy ounces. Unlike a share, it pays no cash flow; its appeal often rises when investors seek liquidity, diversification, protection from currency weakness, or a haven during uncertainty.

Spot gold, shown as XAU/USD, references immediate settlement. Futures such as COMEX GC are standardized contracts for future delivery, commonly used for hedging and leveraged trading.

Brent and WTI vs. gold

Oil benchmarks price energy supply and demand. Gold’s core drivers are monetary conditions, investment demand, physical demand, and perceived systemic risk.

Spot vs. futures

Spot tracks immediate delivery. Futures add expiry, margin, contract specifications, rollover, and the possibility of a basis difference from spot.

Physical, ETF, miner, or derivative?

Physical gold carries storage and dealer costs. ETFs offer liquid exposure, miners add business and equity-market risk, while derivatives add leverage and contract complexity.

ARCHIVE

Five decades of gold shocks

Major moves are rarely explained by a single headline. Monetary policy, inflation, liquidity, and sentiment interact over time.

1971

End of Bretton Woods

Gold convertibility ended, allowing the modern floating-price era to begin.

1979–80

Inflation shock

Oil stress, high inflation, and weak confidence powered a historic surge.

1999–2001

Multi-year trough

Strong growth and low inflation weighed on a prolonged gold bear market.

2008

Global financial crisis

Initial liquidations gave way to powerful safe-haven and monetary-response demand.

2011

Post-crisis peak

Euro-area stress and unconventional policy carried gold to a then-record high.

2013

Taper shock correction

Rising real-rate expectations triggered a sharp repricing lower.

2020

Pandemic record

Extreme uncertainty and low yields pushed gold to fresh highs.

2022–24

Rates, war, central banks

Rate volatility competed with exceptional official-sector demand and geopolitical risk.

DRIVER MATRIX

What moves gold?

Relationships are tendencies, not guarantees. Context and the market’s prior expectations matter.

Typically bearish

U.S. dollar strength

A stronger dollar can make bullion less affordable for non-dollar buyers.

Typically bearish

Higher real yields

Higher inflation-adjusted returns on bonds raise gold’s opportunity cost.

Typically bullish

Central-bank buying

Official reserve diversification can underpin long-cycle physical demand.

Typically bullish

Geopolitical conflict

Risk events can lift demand for perceived liquidity and safety.

Context dependent

Inflation

Inflation can help gold, especially when real yields remain contained.

Typically bullish

ETF inflows

Investment inflows often reinforce price momentum and available demand.

Context dependent

Recession risk

Risk aversion supports gold, but a rush for dollars may offset it initially.

Typically bullish

Physical demand

Jewelry, bar, and coin demand—especially in Asia—can support the market.

Context dependent

Mining supply

New output responds slowly; disruptions can tighten the physical balance.

Typically bullish

Equity volatility

Market stress can increase interest in diversifiers and safe havens.

TRADER PLAYBOOK

Learn to frame the trade

A framework for research—not a prediction engine or recommendation.

01 / TECHNICALS

Read structure first

Mark higher highs and higher lows, then identify support, resistance, trendlines, moving averages, and breakout levels. A setup needs a clear invalidation point.

02 / MACRO

Watch the real-yield lens

Compare gold with real yields and DXY. A rally with falling real yields can differ fundamentally from one driven only by short-term risk headlines.

03 / RISK

Size for uncertainty

Define loss before entry, avoid over-leverage, and account for event risk around CPI, jobs reports, central-bank meetings, and geopolitical headlines.
